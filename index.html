      Book text

      Do I need to do anything here?

      No. The 1939 first edition already came with the app. This is only for loading a different copy — a cleaner transcription, or one that restores the Appendix Dover left out.

      Your notes survive the swap: each one finds its passage by the words it was written against, not by position.

      Later editions (2nd, 3rd and 4th) are still under copyright to A.A. World Services, so please do not import those unless you have permission.

      How this works

      Picking up where you stopped

      Your place is saved four times over: the moment you open a chapter, continuously as you scroll, when you leave the reader, and when you switch away from the app. The last two matter on iPhone, which closes background apps without warning.

      It remembers the paragraph, not just the chapter, so a long chapter picks up where you actually were. The Continue reading card on the Read tab shows it, with how far through the book you are.

      Notes, and how they stay put

      Tap any paragraph while reading and choose Add note. The paragraph gets a coloured edge and the first words of your note appear beneath it.

      Each note quietly stores the opening words of the passage it was written against. If you ever load a different copy of the text, notes find their way back to the right paragraph by those words rather than by position — so they do not drift when paragraph numbering shifts.

      If a passage genuinely disappears from a new copy, its note is flagged on the Notes tab rather than left pointing at the wrong words. Nothing is deleted.

      Things to bring up with your sponsor

      Some things you want to remember are not about a passage at all. Tap + at the top of the Notes tab and write one down — a question that came up on the drive home, something to raise with a sponsee, or a reflection that belongs to nobody but you.

      Anything you write, whether it came off a page or not, can be marked Bring this up with — my sponsor or my sponsee. It then waits on that list, and the count on the filter tells you how many points are still waiting. Notes marked this way also say so in the margin while you are reading, so you meet them again on the page.

      After the conversation, tap Mark as talked about. The point drops to the bottom of the list with the date on it rather than disappearing — what you have already been through is worth keeping. Tap again if there turns out to be more to say.

      Copy this list puts everything still waiting on the clipboard, ready to paste into a message before a call.

      Reflections gathers everything you wrote that did not come from a page, whoever it is for.

      Working the twelve steps

      The Steps tab holds a page for each of the twelve. Every page carries the step as the 1939 edition prints it, a plain explanation of what it asks, the passages of the book that describe it — several, where the book makes its case in more than one place — and questions to take to your sponsor.

      Answering a question keeps every earlier answer. Answer it again a year later and the new one sits on top, dated, with the old ones folded underneath. Nothing you have written is overwritten, here or anywhere else in the app. Questions you have no use for can be put away, which never touches what you wrote against them, and you can add your own.

      Notes on this step are separate from the answers: a note each time you read or work the step, newest first, long ones folded until you open them.

      Below that is the work of that particular step, and it differs by step because the steps do. Step four has the three inventory tables the book describes. Step eight builds the amends list and step nine records onto the same list rather than making a second one. Step six asks about the defects step four named, once each however many times you wrote them. Steps ten and eleven are a daily rhythm rather than a piece of work, so they show the last fortnight. Step five keeps a record that the telling happened, and step twelve who you have sat with.

      The list of twelve shows what you have done on each: how many questions you have answered, how many notes you have written, how much of that step's own work is there, and the day you last worked it. A step with nothing written says nothing rather than showing you a row of noughts.

      Taking a step out of the app

      Copy this step out, at the bottom of any step page, turns what you have written into plain text you can paste into a message or an email before a call.

      You tick what goes: your answers, your notes, that step's work, and whether the earlier answers travel with the latest. The whole text is shown to you before anything is copied. This is the only place in the app where what you have written can leave your phone, and it should never be a surprise what went.

      Step five's what I held back is the exception that proves it: the page keeps it folded away, and it is left out of a copy unless you tick it by name. It starts unticked every time.

      Nothing is sent anywhere by the app itself. It goes on the clipboard, and where it goes after that is your doing.

      Bookmarks and search

      Tap a paragraph and choose Bookmark this passage to mark it without writing anything.

      Search covers every paragraph in the book. Tap a result to land on that passage with it briefly highlighted. It stops at 200 matches, so narrow the phrase if you are searching for a common word.

      Making it comfortable to read

      The Aa button in the top-right of the reader changes size, spacing and theme without leaving the page. The same controls, plus the choice of serif or sans-serif, are under Reading above.

      Match device follows your phone's light and dark setting. Keep screen awake stops the display dimming while you read, where the browser allows it.

      Backups, and moving to a new phone

      A backup is one JSON file holding your notes, bookmarks, reading position and settings. On iPhone Create backup opens the share sheet — save it to Files or iCloud Drive, or mail it to yourself.

      On the new phone, install the app, then Choose backup file. Pick Replace everything on a fresh install, or Keep both to merge, where the newer copy of any note wins.

      Leave Include the book text unticked and the file stays under a kilobyte, because the app already ships with the text. Tick it only if you have imported a copy of your own.

      Working offline

      The whole book and the whole app are stored on your device the first time you open them. After that nothing is fetched — aeroplanes, basements and lifts all work the same as anywhere else.

      Add it to your home screen and it runs full-screen with no browser bar. On iPhone: Share → Add to Home Screen.

      Where your reading lives

      Everything — notes, bookmarks, your place, your settings — is stored on this device only. There is no account, no server, no analytics, and no network request at all once the app has loaded. Nobody can see what you have written, including whoever made this.

      The other side of that: if you delete the app, or clear this site's data in your browser settings, it is all gone and cannot be recovered. That is what backups are for. Making one now and then costs a few seconds.

      Version history
      The craving log and the three days2.18

      The two screens are joined up. An entry that ends in a drink is exactly the moment nobody wants to go hunting through an app, so the page for the days after is offered there and then.

      • Offered once, and it takes a no. Save a craving as having ended in a drink and the three days are offered. Say not now and nothing more is said about it.
      • A quiet row on the craving screen for three days afterwards, so a no is not a door closing. After that it goes on its own rather than sitting there as a reproach.
      • The date is carried over rather than asked for twice — and is still yours to change, along with whether to count your days again.
      • It remembers where it came from. The three days link back to the entry, and what you wrote at the time can go into the copy for your sponsor under "What led to it" — the only part of that text written before the event rather than after it.

      Nothing about this appears on the front page. The app noticing is one thing; the app bringing it up every morning is another.

      Starting again2.17

      If abstinence breaks, there is now a page for the three days after it, reached from a quiet row under the shortcuts. Nothing on it scolds — somebody opening it has already had the worst of it.

      • Three days, written out, each with things to do that you tick off and somewhere to write. Day one leads with the doctor: coming off drink can be dangerous, and this app is not the thing that helps with that.
      • The book on the same page — The Doctor's Opinion, More About Alcoholism, How It Works, one tap each.
      • It asks, rather than assumes. The date, because by the time anyone opens this it may be yesterday; and whether to count your days again from then, because it is your count and not the app's.
      • Copy it out for your sponsor, showing what is done and what is not — the point of showing somebody is that they can see the gaps.
      • When the three days are done you close it, and what you wrote is kept.

      The instructions are mine to suggest and yours to rewrite. They are not A.A. material and do not pretend to be.

      Copying the meetings out2.16

      Copy these out, at the foot of the meeting page: where you have been, as plain text. The last thirty days by default, or all of them; what was worth keeping from each; and what is still waiting to be brought up, if you tick it.

      The two copy sheets are one sheet now — the same tick-what-goes, read-it-first shape wherever it turns up. Copying a step out keeps its own, which does more.

      Taking the page to the meeting2.15

      Copy this page out, at the foot of either conversation page, turns the day into plain text for a message before the call or after it. You see the whole thing before anything is copied, and the notes from the meeting can be left out with a tick.

      What you have not answered is counted and said at the end rather than left as a silence — the same as copying a step out. Nothing is sent by the app; it goes on the clipboard, and where it goes after that is your doing.

      Before we talk2.14

      I'm meeting my sponsor and I'm meeting my sponsee now open a page a day for that conversation, instead of just the list of points waiting.

      Each has its own questions, because one is about you and the other is about them — Am I abstinent? What have I done for myself today? For others? Do I hide anything? How do I feel? Any questions? — and on the sponsee's side the same asked of him, with Don't know allowed, because sometimes you do not.

      Notes from the meeting closes both, since what you want tomorrow is what was actually said. Everything saves as you leave the field: there is no Save button to forget when the phone rings.

      What you have marked to bring up is on the same page, ready to copy out, and the days already written are underneath — tap one to read it back.

      Days running counts showing up2.13

      Days running on the home screen counted days you had written a step ten entry. It counts days you have opened the app now — showing up is the thing being counted, and it is a lower bar on purpose.

      It did not start from nothing: every day you already had something dated — a note, a chapter read, a craving, a meeting — is a day the app was open, and those were counted in when this arrived. Tap the tile and it tells you your longest run.

      The step ten and step eleven runs have not gone anywhere; they are still on those step pages, where they are about that step's own practice.

      A passing look stops counting2.12

      A fault, now fixed. Reading today's passage moved Continue reading to it, and the percentage with it. An extra reading in the middle of the day is not where you are up to.

      Going to a single passage — today's passage, a step's reference, the passage behind a note, a search hit — no longer touches your place, and the reader says Not keeping your place while you are there. If you decide to carry on reading from there, one tap on that strip makes it count from wherever you have got to.

      It ends the moment you leave the reader, so the next chapter you open from the contents is remembered as usual. Opening a whole chapter was always reading and still counts.

      A little emphasis in the rules2.11

      The rules take a bit of formatting now. **Two stars** makes a phrase bold, *one star* or _an underscore_ makes it italic. Everything else is left exactly as you typed it.

      You still write them in a plain box, one to a line. A proper rich-text editor — a toolbar, buttons for bold and italic — is a lot of fragile machinery on a phone for the sake of making one word heavier, and this does the same job in two keystrokes.

      Reading without keeping your place2.10

      A third thing behind the three dots on Continue reading: read without keeping your place. For when you want to spend an hour somewhere in the book without the app deciding that is where you are up to.

      Nothing is written while it is on — not on opening a chapter, not as you scroll, not on leaving. The card stays exactly where it was, however far you wander. The reader says so at the top the whole time, and one tap there puts you back to keeping your place, from wherever you have got to.

      It also switches itself off when the app is next started, so it cannot quietly stay on for a fortnight.

      Two lists of rules, in Settings2.9

      The rules are two lists now — with my sponsor and with my sponsee — because they are two different conversations: what you keep, and what you have asked of someone else.

      They have moved to Settings → Rules and off the home screen. A rule you have read for a fortnight does not need reading again every morning. Anything already written stays where it was, on the sponsor list.

      The rules, and adjusting where you are2.8

      The rules — the ones you have agreed, with a sponsor or with a sponsee — now sit on the home screen under the day count. Short enough to read every day without deciding to, which is the point of having them written down at all. Tap them to change them: one to a line. Empty the list and the card goes.

      Continue reading can be adjusted. The three dots in the corner of the card offer to start that chapter again, or to forget where you are altogether — which takes the card away and offers the book from the beginning. Neither touches your notes or your bookmarks. Moving it somewhere else entirely is still a matter of opening that chapter from the contents, which is what sets it.

      Whether you have been here, and who to ring2.7

      Under the day count, a line saying how long it is since you actually read or wrote something in here. Opening the app does not count — standing in the doorway is not the practice. It stays grey for a day or two, takes the accent colour after that, and goes red past a week.

      The day count itself is not coloured, on purpose. A low number there is not a warning, and an app that turns somebody's third day red is scolding them.

      Sponsee and spouse now sit alongside your sponsor in Settings → People you can ring. Whoever has a number is a tap away at the top of the craving screen — the book's own answer to a shaky evening is to work with another alcoholic, so the sponsee belongs on that list.

      Counting the days2.6

      A day count at the top of the home screen. Set the first day in Settings → Counting the days and it counts inclusively, so the day you set it reads 1 day — which is how anybody says it, and day one is the one worth counting most.

      Tap it to change the date. Leave the date empty and the counter is not there at all; there is no nought on the home screen.

      A 24-hour clock, and the version on the tab2.5

      No a.m. or p.m. anywhere any more — the clock on the home screen, the time a craving started, the date on a note. All of it reads 18:35.

      And the version you are running now sits under the Settings tab, small enough to ignore until you go looking for it.

      The version history folds away2.4

      Twenty-seven releases is a lot of screen to scroll past on the way to anything below it, and most days you do not want any of it. The whole history is one line now, with the version you are running on the end of it. Everything inside still opens one at a time, as before.

      Meetings, and the last placeholder2.3

      I'm going to a meeting was the last tile with nothing behind it. Every shortcut on the home screen now goes somewhere.

      • To bring up — a note can now be waiting for a meeting, alongside your sponsor and your sponsee. Mark one while you are reading, or write one down here, and the list is ready to copy out before you go.
      • A record that you went — the day, which one, whether you spoke, and anything worth keeping. Nobody remembers in March how many they got to in January, so the screen counts them: how many in all, how many in the last thirty days, and how many you spoke at.
      • The meetings you already go to come up as taps rather than being typed again — which also keeps the same meeting under the same name, and that is what makes the list worth counting.
      • The home tile says when the last one was.
      The craving screen2.2

      I have cravings on the home screen was a place held open. It has something behind it now.

      • A record, kept while it is happening. One tap starts a clock; one tap closes it. In the middle of a craving it is not obvious that it will end, and the only convincing argument that it will is the list of the ones that did — so the list leads with how many, and how long the longest ran.
      • It can say you drank. A list that only records victories is not a record, and step ten asks for the other kind too. The count then stops claiming every one passed, because it would not be true.
      • Ring your sponsor, if you have put their number in Settings — it stays on this phone like everything else, and is used for nothing but that button. Write down what is going on puts a note on the list for them. And More About Alcoholism is one tap away, being the chapter on exactly this.
      • A passage from the book, drawn from eleven chosen for this moment and weighted towards what to do rather than what is wrong with you. Verified word for word, the same as the daily one.
      • The home tile carries where things stand — None for six days, which is the number that grows while nothing is happening.
      A front page2.1

      The app used to open in the contents, as though reading were the only reason you ever picked it up. It opens on a home screen now.

      • Today's passage — one passage from the book, the same one all day, changing at midnight. Tap it to read it where it sits in the chapter. Ninety-four of them, so one comes round again about every three months, and every one is checked word for word against the 1939 text before it ships: nothing on that card was typed from memory.
      • The time and the date, at the top, and a word for the hour it is.
      • What brings you here — the handful of reasons you actually opened the app, in your own words rather than the app's. Two of them have nothing behind them yet and say so when tapped: a place held open is more honest than a button that quietly does nothing.
      • Where you have got to — how far through the book, what you have written, how many steps you have worked on, how many days you are running on a daily step. Each count is of exactly what its own screen shows, and a morning with nothing written says so.
      • The contents is now the Read tab, unchanged otherwise, and still remembers your place.
      The steps section, finished2.0

      All twelve steps, each with the work that step actually asks for — and two things the section was still missing.

      • The list was undercounting you. It counted notes and nothing else, so answering eight questions and filling in an inventory left the row reading as untouched. Answers, notes and work now count together, each kind of work named in its own words — 2 sittings, 1 person, 6 entries — with the day you last worked it.
      • Copy this step out, at the foot of every step page. You tick what goes, you see the whole text before anything is copied, and it goes on the clipboard rather than anywhere else.
      • Step five's what I held back is left out of that copy unless you ask for it by name, and starts unticked every time. The page folds it away; a copy button should not quietly undo that.
      • A fault found while building it: the sheets that slide up from the bottom were closed by name, from a list five long, so a sheet added later stayed open behind whatever came next. They all close now, however many there are.
      All twelve have their work1.22

      Step twelve keeps who you have sat with, and what came of it. Every one of the twelve steps now has a page built for what that step actually asks.

      • Each person is dated from when you first sat down, with how it came about and what happened.
      • The count leads, because it is easy to forget how many there have been and easy to remember only the ones that went badly.
      • Still in touch, not in touch, or do not know — which is the commonest answer and is not treated as a failure. Someone can be written down before you know how it went.
      The defects step four named1.21

      Step six carries through what you wrote in step four, so you answer about your own defects rather than about defects in general. Eleven of twelve steps now have their work.

      • It reads two of step four's columns — the part you played in a resentment, and the fault behind a piece of conduct.
      • A defect written several times is asked about once, and says how often it came up and in which lists. Seeing that a thing was written five times is itself the answer to some of them.
      • Each carries an honest answer rather than a tick, and can be marked ready or still held on to. Untouched is its own state — the page does not flatter you by defaulting to ready.
      • Entries are carried whole and never split on punctuation. What you wrote is what you are asked about.
      A record of the telling1.20

      Step five keeps a record of each sitting. Ten of twelve steps now have their own work.

      • A sitting is dated by the day it happened, not the day you typed it up, and records who you sat with and roughly what you covered.
      • Going back later to say the thing you left out is a second sitting, not an edit of the first. Both keep their dates.
      • What you held back is folded away until you ask for it, and folds itself away again whenever the page is reopened. That is discretion rather than security — the app still has no lock.
      Two lists, and things that move1.19

      Steps one and two keep two lists each, added to over time rather than filled in once. Nine of twelve steps now have their own work.

      • Step one holds evidence on both halves — what happened once you drank, and what was coming apart whether or not you did.
      • Step two holds what you cannot accept yet, and what has shifted. Anything can be moved across, and it keeps the date it was first written, so the page shows that your mind changed and roughly when.
      • A blank entry is refused, in lists whose whole purpose is evidence.
      A date on the decision1.18

      Steps three and seven have their own work now — seven of twelve steps still to go.

      • Each shows its passage from the book, with a link to read it in place, and records the dates you took the step.
      • Every date is kept, newest first, so the page shows the years rather than only the latest. An earlier date can be added, and a note kept against any of them.
      • The same day twice is refused rather than quietly duplicated.
      Keeping a daily practice1.17

      Steps ten and eleven are daily, not one long piece of work, so they now have a shape of their own.

      • A strip of the last fortnight sits at the top of each. Days you have written on are marked, and the current run is counted.
      • Step ten offers watchwords to check yourself against, and a day can carry more than one at a time.
      • Step eleven asks after its three parts — and a part-filled day saves, rather than demanding all three before it will record anything.
      • A day missed can be filled in later from the strip, and counts towards the run when it is.
      • The two steps keep their days apart, and every entry is dated and carried in the backup like everything else.
      The amends list1.16

      Steps eight and nine, built as one list you see twice.

      • Step eight holds the names and the harm, each with where your willingness actually stands. Anyone you named in step four's conduct pass is offered to it, so you are not typing them twice.
      • Step nine shows the same entries with what happened — planned, made, letter sent, withheld because it would injure someone, or cannot be put right — with the date you choose rather than the day you typed it.
      • They write to one row, not two. Step nine can never remove a name, and deleting one that carries step nine's record asks first.
      • An entry is never taken off for being impossible. Withheld and cannot be put right are answers the list keeps.
      All twelve written1.15

      Steps ten, eleven and twelve, and with them the last of the stubs.

      • Continued — where the book stops describing steps you take and starts describing ones you practise: four things to watch, a sequence to run at once rather than at the weekend, and a daily reprieve that is not a cure.
      • Prayer and meditation — the routine in three parts, morning, the pause during the day, and the evening review. That review sits under step eleven in this edition although it reads like step ten, and the page says so rather than quietly moving it.
      • Carried the message — the twelfth suggestion put in terms of your own survival, what it costs, and the restraint the book is equally firm about: do not chase anyone, and we have no monopoly on God. It ends where the book ends.
      • Eighty-three passages now resolve across all twelve steps, and every one of them has been checked against the text rather than remembered.
      One word of the text corrected1.14

      A conversion artifact in the promises, fixed at the source.

      • Into Action read "Wé are going to know a new freedom and happiness". It now reads We. The stray accent came from converting the EPUB, not from the printed page.
      • The text was corrected in the source file and the book rebuilt from it, rather than patched in the app — so the word count still reconciles exactly against the source, at 106,067 of 106,067.
      • Nothing else in the text changed. The two remaining accents, protegé and fiancée, are the book's own.
      Steps eight and nine1.13

      The amends, which the book pairs itself and then spends most of a chapter on.

      • The list — six passages, on where the names already are (step four made most of this list), what counts as harm, and what to do when the willingness is not there: ask until it comes, and leave the name on.
      • Amends — seven passages, including how to conduct it (we stick to our own), the except-when clause worked through with a real case, whose consent you need first, the ones that can never be put right, and the promises the book prints at the end of it.
      • Both declare their work as one list seen twice: the names and the harm in step eight, the same entries with status and what happened in step nine — which is kept for years rather than finished. Nothing renders those yet.
      Steps six and seven1.12

      The two shortest steps in the book, written short on purpose.

      • Entirely ready and Humbly asked get four passages each rather than the usual seven. The 1939 text gives them 134 words between them, and padding that out would have meant writing commentary and calling it the book.
      • Step six points back at your own step four rather than at failings in general, and carries the line for when you are still clinging to something.
      • Step seven keeps the prayer with a date each time it is asked, the way step three does — and carries the book's own warning that defects do not disappear overnight.
      The step four inventory1.11

      Step four's three tables are built, and you can fill them in.

      • Resentments, fears and conduct, with the columns the book names — who you are resentful at, the cause, what it affects, and where you were to blame.
      • Two views of the same rows. Fill in gives you one entry at a time with its fields stacked, which is the view to dictate into. Read back lays them out side by side the way the book prints its example, scrolling sideways inside its own box so the page never does.
      • Copy this table puts a whole pass into plain text, to take to whoever is going to hear it.
      • An entry with nothing in it is refused rather than saved, and every row is carried by the backup — so the inventory moves to a new phone with everything else.
      Steps four and five1.10

      The two steps most people slow down on, written up together, because the second is only as good as the first.

      • Inventory — seven passages: the shop taking stock, resentment as the number one offender and the line where the book calls it fatal, the turn to your own part, fear as the corroding thread, and the questions the third pass asks.
      • Told someone — seven passages: why a solitary self-appraisal is not enough, how the book weighs up who should hear it, the limit on what you may put onto someone it would injure, and the arch you are asked to inspect afterwards.
      • The step as printed says the exact nature of our wrongs; the chapter that takes it up again says defects. That is the 1939 text, not a transcription slip, and the step page says so.
      • Both steps declare the work they will carry — three inventory tables on four, a dated record of the telling on five. Nothing renders those yet.
      Steps two and three1.9

      Two more steps written up, and a navigation fix they turned up.

      • Came to believe — six passages, including the one short question the book reduces the whole step to, and the place where it declines to tell you what to believe.
      • Turned it over — five passages: the actor trying to run the whole show, the eleven words the turn is made in, and the prayer as the 1939 edition prints it.
      • Opening a passage from a step and then leaving the reader now returns you to that step, rather than to the Read tab. The same applies coming from Notes or Search.
      Answering the questions1.8

      The questions on a step can be answered now, and answering one again does not lose what you said the first time.

      • The latest answer is shown under its question; earlier ones are kept, dated, and open on request. Working a step a second time is not a correction of the first.
      • A question that does not match how you were taught can be put away — and anything already written against it is kept, so bringing it back brings the answers with it.
      • You can add questions of your own, however your sponsor puts them.
      • Answers are notes like any other: searchable, markable for your sponsor, and carried in the same backup — along with the questions you added or put away.
      Notes that fold1.7

      Dictate a long note and it no longer buries the step it belongs to.

      • A note too long to sit comfortably in the page is folded to a few lines and opens on a tap. A short one is left alone rather than given a control that does nothing.
      • Your journal is now Notes on this step, to match Note on this passage in the reader — the same thing said the same way.
      • Each note carries its own Edit, and marking one as talked about now works from the step page as well as the Notes tab.
      A journal on every step1.6

      Every step now takes dated entries, added each time you read or work it.

      • Entries are kept, never replaced. A second pass sits above the first, with both dates intact.
      • They are notes like any other, so an entry can be marked for your sponsor, searched beside your book notes, and carried in the same backup.
      • A new Steps filter on the Notes tab gathers everything written against a step; Reflections now means what did not come from a page or a step.
      • Steps you have written against show a count, and a step still awaiting its write-up takes entries all the same — its wording is there to work with.
      The twelve steps arrive1.5

      A fifth tab, and the beginning of a section for working the steps.

      • All twelve are listed, each showing its wording as the 1939 edition prints it. Step one is written up; the rest show their wording and are marked as still to come.
      • A step page carries a plain explanation of what the step asks, links into the book, and questions to take to a sponsor. Step one has seven links — the book makes its case for this one across three different chapters.
      • Every link is stored by the opening words of the passage, not by a paragraph number, so it still opens the right place if you ever load a different copy of the text. A passage that genuinely cannot be found says so instead of opening the wrong one.
      • The explanations and questions were written for this app. They are not official A.A. material, and they are meant to be disagreed with.
      Things to talk about1.4

      The Notes tab now holds more than notes on passages.

      • Write a note of your own with the + button — for the things that do not come out of a page.
      • Bring this up with my sponsor / my sponsee. Any note can be marked for a conversation, whether it started on a page or not, and the filters at the top of the tab keep a count of what is still waiting.
      • Mark as talked about after the conversation. The point stays, dated, at the bottom of the list rather than vanishing.
      • Copy this list hands you everything still waiting, ready to paste into a message before a call.
      • A note kept for someone says so in the margin while you read, so you come across it again on the page and not only in a list.
      Explaining itself1.3

      Two new sections in Settings, and the fix that writing them uncovered.

      • How this works — what the app does that you cannot see: that your place is saved at four separate moments, that notes find their passage by the words they were written against rather than by position, what a backup actually contains, and that clearing this site's data erases everything because nothing is stored anywhere else.
      • Version history — this list, recording the faults found along the way as well as the features added.
      • The Book text panel had been offering to remove imported text even when the text was the copy that came with the app and nothing had been imported. Harmless if tapped, but alarming to read. That button now appears only for a copy you supplied yourself, and the panel leads with the fact that nothing needs doing there.
      Hand-drawn icons1.2

      All eight icons in the tab bar and the passage menu were redrawn by hand.

      • They had been a mix of colour emoji and plain text glyphs, so half rendered in colour and half as flat type, and none could follow the theme.
      • They are now line drawings that take the accent colour in every theme, including dark, and stay sharp at any size.
      • The chosen tab's icon gives a small hop, suppressed if your phone has Reduce Motion switched on.
      The 1939 text1.1

      The complete first edition now ships with the app: 43 sections, 1,453 paragraphs, 106,067 words — the Foreword, The Doctor's Opinion, all eleven chapters and all twenty-nine personal stories. Nothing to import before reading.

      Where it came from. The 2011 Dover republication of the 1939 edition. Dover's own 2011 introduction and biographical notes are still under copyright and are excluded; only the 1939 text is here. Dover's edition also omits the original two-page Appendix, which is why a footnote closing A Vision For You points at something that is not there.

      Three faults this uncovered, each one losing or inventing text:

      • Two chapters were being split apart by lines of prose mistaken for headings — a capitalised question in We Agnostics and an attribution in An Artist's Concept — and those lines were being swallowed. Headings are now marked explicitly rather than guessed at.
      • The Foreword's closing signature was being deleted, because a filter meant to strip page headers matched it exactly.
      • Chapter openings were shouting — RARELY have we seen a person fail — where the printed book uses small capitals. They now read normally, while genuine capitals are left alone.

      Every build is now reconciled against its source word by word. This one is exact, with nothing dropped.

      First release1.0

      The reader, notes, bookmarks, search, backups and offline support.

      • Four themes, serif or sans-serif, adjustable size and line spacing.
      • Reading position tracked per paragraph. An early version only saved while scrolling, so a chapter short enough to fit on one screen was never remembered at all; it now saves on opening, on leaving, and on the app being sent to the background.
      • Notes and bookmarks anchored to a passage by its opening words, so they survive the text being reloaded in another format.
      • Full-text search across the book.
      • One-file backup and restore, using the iPhone share sheet, since a plain download is unreliable inside an installed web app.
      • Runs entirely offline once installed.
